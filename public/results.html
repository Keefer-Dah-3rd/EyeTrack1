How the scores are worked out
  • What gets measured. Hierarchy, Readability and two of the Density measures read the page's own styles, so they only work for pages on the same address as this tool. The other two Density measures read the captured screen, so they need the heat map option on. Gaze focus comes from the sessions selected above and needs at least seconds of gaze on the page.
  • Keep the window the same size. Words per screenful and line length change with the window, so only compare pages recorded at the same size.
  • Where the targets come from. Contrast, line length and line spacing use WCAG accessibility targets. Every other scale is this tool's own, set by measuring eight public websites on 31 October 2026. They are not industry norms and haven't been tested against real task success.
  • What the scores can't tell you. They don't know what you meant to be most important, so a clear hierarchy can still point at the wrong thing. Text inside images isn't counted, and text over an image is left out of the contrast check. High density can be right for expert tools.
  • Gaze focus. It shows how much gaze gathered in a few places, not whether those were the right places. A task that sends people around the page will lower it.
  • Pages that changed. Layout scores use the newest session that visited a page. To see an older version of a page, pick that session above.
