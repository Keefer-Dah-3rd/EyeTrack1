Why good notes get lost

Most people write down far more than they ever read again. The trouble usually isn't the writing. It's what happens in the weeks after.

The problem with folders

Folders ask you to decide where something belongs at the very moment you know the least about how you will need it later. A note about a supplier might belong with a project, a person, a budget or a reminder for next spring. Whichever one you pick, the other three become places you will look for it and not find it. After a few months of this, most people stop filing altogether and simply search, which works only if they can remember the words they used at the time.

Timing matters more than tidiness

A note is most useful at the moment it becomes relevant again, and that moment is rarely the one you would have predicted. The pricing idea you had in March matters when the contract arrives in June. The advice from a colleague matters on the morning you walk into the meeting with them. Tidy storage does nothing for this. What helps is something that notices the connection and puts the right note in front of you while there is still time to use it.

Writing less, but keeping more

When people trust that a note will come back at the right time, they tend to write shorter ones. They stop copying whole documents just in case, and they stop rewriting the same thought in three different places. A short note in your own words is also easier to recognise when it does come back, because it carries a little of what you were thinking when you wrote it. In our own testing, people kept writing for longer once they stopped worrying about where everything should go.

Where to start

Pick one kind of note you already take, such as meeting notes, and leave everything else alone for a month. Write them wherever is quickest, keep them short, and add a word or two about when you might want them again. At the end of the month, count how many came back when you needed them. That number will tell you far more than any system comparison, and it will show you what to try next without having to reorganise anything at all.
